ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The proliferation of misinformation on digital platforms poses an unprecedented
challenge to public discourse, health, and democratic institutions. Existing
automated fact-checking systems are predominantly binary classifiers that label
content as simply "fake" or "real," failing to capture the nuanced spectrum of
inaccuracy present in real-world media.

This report presents Veritas AI, a hybrid, three-class misinformation detection
system designed to classify textual content into: Highly Deceptive (conspiracy-
level fabrication), Misleading (exaggerated or selectively framed), and
Legitimate (credible and well-sourced). The system combines a fine-tuned
DistilBERT transformer model with 152 hand-crafted linguistic deception patterns
and an optional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) fact-checking pipeline.

Training was conducted on approximately 51,000 samples drawn from the Kaggle
Fake/Real News dataset, the LIAR benchmark (PolitiFact), and synthetically
generated data. The model employs Focal Loss to address class imbalance,
differential learning rates for stable fine-tuning, and early stopping based
on macro F1 score. A continuous learning pipeline automatically fetches live
news and retrains the model on a scheduled basis.

The system is deployed as a REST API built with FastAPI, fronted by a responsive
HTML/CSS/JS web interface, and containerised with Docker. The best model (v6)
achieves a macro F1 score of 0.9008, demonstrating strong generalisation across
all three severity classes.

Keywords: Misinformation Detection, DistilBERT, Focal Loss, RAG, FAISS,
NLP, Fake News, Transformer Fine-Tuning, FastAPI, Continuous Learning

1.1 BACKGROUND OF THE PROJECT TOPIC
--------------------------------------

The digital information age has fundamentally changed how news and knowledge
are produced, distributed, and consumed. Social media platforms, news aggregators,
messaging applications, and blogs have democratised content creation, enabling
billions of users to produce and share information without editorial oversight.
While this democratisation has numerous benefits, it has simultaneously created
an environment where misinformation — false, inaccurate, or deliberately
misleading information — can spread rapidly at global scale.

Misinformation is not a monolithic phenomenon. Researchers have identified
several distinct categories:

  Disinformation:  Deliberately fabricated content intended to deceive.
                   Example: fabricated statistics attributed to government studies.

  Malinformation:  True information used out of context to harm or mislead.
                   Example: a real photograph from a different country used to
                   misrepresent current events.

  Misinformation:  False content shared without harmful intent, often due to
                   misunderstanding or lack of verification.
                   Example: medical myths shared in good faith.

  Propaganda:      Biased information used to promote a particular agenda.

The consequences of widespread misinformation are well-documented. During the
COVID-19 pandemic, health misinformation — including claims about unproven
cures, vaccine side effects, and the pandemic's origin — contributed to vaccine
hesitancy, inappropriate self-treatment, and unnecessary public panic. The World
Health Organization formally declared an "infodemic" alongside the pandemic.
Similarly, misinformation has been linked to electoral interference, ethnic
violence, and public distrust of legitimate institutions.

Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Machine Learning (ML) offer a promising
path toward scalable, automated misinformation detection. Transformer-based
language models such as BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from
Transformers), introduced by Devlin et al. in 2018, have set new benchmarks
across a wide range of NLP tasks. Their ability to capture deep contextual
relationships between words makes them particularly well-suited to detecting
subtle linguistic signals of deception, exaggeration, and bias.

DistilBERT (Sanh et al., 2019) is a distilled version of BERT that retains
97% of BERT's language understanding capabilities while being 40% smaller and
60% faster — making it practical for deployment in real-time applications.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), introduced by Lewis et al. (2020),
combines dense retrieval with generative language models to ground AI responses
in factual source documents. Applied to misinformation detection, RAG enables
the system to compare a suspicious claim against a database of verified facts,
providing evidence-based verdicts rather than pattern-matching alone.

1.2 MOTIVATION AND SCOPE OF THE REPORT
----------------------------------------

Motivation:

  The primary motivation for this project is the inadequacy of existing binary
  misinformation classifiers for real-world deployment. Labeling a news article
  as simply "fake" or "real" is often insufficient:

    - A technically true statement can be deeply misleading through selective
      framing or misleading context.
    - Not all false information is equally dangerous — a satirical article
      misread as fact is different from a fabricated medical claim.
    - Content moderation decisions need to be proportional to severity.

  Furthermore, most published systems are evaluated on static datasets and do
  not account for the dynamic nature of misinformation, which evolves rapidly.
  A system deployed today will face linguistic patterns and topics not present
  in its training data within weeks.

  This project was motivated by the desire to build a system that is:
    1. Severity-aware (three classes, not two)
    2. Explainable (provides human-readable reasons)
    3. Continuously updated (live news ingestion + scheduled retraining)
    4. Practically deployable (REST API, web interface, Docker support)
    5. Evidence-grounded when possible (RAG pipeline)

Scope:

  This report covers:
    - The complete design and implementation of the Veritas AI system
    - Dataset construction from multiple sources
    - DistilBERT fine-tuning with advanced training techniques
    - The RAG pipeline architecture and integration
    - REST API and web frontend design
    - Continuous learning infrastructure
    - Performance evaluation against established benchmarks
    - Compliance analysis against IEEE standards

  Out of scope:
    - Non-English text classification
    - Audio/video misinformation detection
    - Real-time social media stream monitoring (planned future work)

1.3 PROBLEM STATEMENT
-----------------------

Existing automated misinformation detection systems suffer from three key
deficiencies:

  P1. Binary Classification Granularity
      Most systems classify content as binary fake/real, ignoring the spectrum
      of severity. This makes them unsuitable for nuanced content moderation
      where different responses are appropriate for different severity levels.

  P2. Static Knowledge
      Models trained on static datasets rapidly become outdated as misinformation
      trends evolve. Without a mechanism for continuous learning, model accuracy
      degrades over time.

  P3. Lack of Explainability
      Black-box classifiers that output only a label and confidence score do not
      provide users with actionable information about why content was flagged,
      reducing trust and adoption.

Proposed Solution:
  Design and implement a hybrid misinformation detection system (Veritas AI) that:
    - Classifies content into three severity levels with actionable labels
    - Combines transformer-based deep learning with rule-based linguistic analysis
    - Includes an evidence-grounded RAG fact-checking pipeline
    - Provides human-readable justifications for each classification
    - Continuously updates its knowledge through live news ingestion and retraining

2.1 INTRODUCTION TO OVERALL TOPIC
------------------------------------

Automatic misinformation detection is a subfield of Natural Language Processing
(NLP) that applies computational methods to identify false, misleading, or
fabricated information in text. The field has grown rapidly since approximately
2017, driven by public concern over "fake news" in political contexts and later
amplified by health misinformation during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The core task is typically framed as text classification: given an article,
headline, or statement, predict its veracity label. This framing has been
tackled using a progression of increasingly powerful methods:

  Era 1 (2017–2018): Feature Engineering + Classical ML
    TF-IDF, n-gram features, POS tags, writing style features fed into
    Support Vector Machines (SVM), Naive Bayes, and Random Forests.

  Era 2 (2018–2019): Deep Learning — RNN/LSTM/CNN
    Word embeddings (Word2Vec, GloVe) + LSTM or CNN architectures for
    sequential text modeling.

  Era 3 (2019–2021): Transfer Learning — BERT and variants
    Pre-trained transformer models fine-tuned on fake news datasets.
    Major leap in performance across all benchmarks.

  Era 4 (2021–present): Multi-modal, Graph, RAG approaches
    Incorporating evidence retrieval, knowledge graphs, claim decomposition,
    and LLM-based reasoning.

The primary benchmarks used in the field include:
  - LIAR (Wang, 2017): 12,836 PolitiFact statements, 6-class rating
  - FakeNewsNet (Shu et al., 2018): News articles with social graph features
  - FEVER (Thorne et al., 2018): Fact Extraction and VERification dataset
  - MultiFC (Augenstein et al., 2019): Multi-domain fact-checking corpus

2.2 LITERATURE SURVEY
-----------------------

[Paper 1]
  Title:   "Liar, Liar Pants on Fire: A New Benchmark Dataset for Fake
            News Detection"
  Authors: Wang, W. Y.
  Year:    2017
  Venue:   ACL 2017

  Summary:
    Introduced the LIAR dataset containing 12,836 manually labeled political
    statements from PolitiFact with six veracity levels: pants-fire, false,
    barely-true, half-true, mostly-true, and true. Wang trained a BERT-like
    model with metadata (speaker, subject, venue) achieving ~27% accuracy on
    the 6-class problem, demonstrating the difficulty of fine-grained
    veracity classification.

  Relevance to this project:
    The LIAR dataset is directly used as one of the three training sources.
    The original 6-class labels are mapped to our 3-class schema.
    This work established that metadata and speaker credibility features
    are valuable signals — insight reflected in our linguistic pattern layer.

[Paper 2]
  Title:   "BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for
            Language Understanding"
  Authors: Devlin, J., Chang, M. W., Lee, K., & Toutanova, K.
  Year:    2018
  Venue:   NAACL 2019

  Summary:
    Introduced BERT, the foundational transformer pre-trained on masked
    language modeling (MLM) and next sentence prediction (NSP) tasks using
    Wikipedia and BookCorpus. BERT achieved state-of-the-art results on 11
    NLP benchmarks. Its bidirectional attention mechanism captures context
    from both left and right simultaneously, unlike previous unidirectional
    language models.

  Relevance to this project:
    DistilBERT, the model used in this project, is a direct derivative of BERT.
    All BERT architectural concepts (attention heads, tokenization, fine-tuning
    paradigm) apply directly to our implementation.

[Paper 3]
  Title:   "DistilBERT, a distilled version of BERT: smaller, faster,
            cheaper and lighter"
  Authors: Sanh, V., Debut, L., Chaumond, J., & Wolf, T.
  Year:    2019
  Venue:   NeurIPS 2019 Workshop

  Summary:
    Introduced DistilBERT, which reduces BERT's size by 40% and increases
    inference speed by 60% using knowledge distillation — training a smaller
    "student" model to mimic the output distribution of the larger BERT
    "teacher." DistilBERT retains 97% of BERT's performance on the GLUE
    benchmark while using far fewer parameters (66M vs 110M).

  Relevance to this project:
    DistilBERT-base-uncased is the backbone model for the Veritas AI
    classifier. Its efficiency makes real-time inference feasible without
    GPU acceleration, enabling deployment on consumer hardware.

[Paper 4]
  Title:   "Focal Loss for Dense Object Detection"
  Authors: Lin, T. Y., Goyal, P., Girshick, R., He, K., & Dollar, P.
  Year:    2017
  Venue:   ICCV 2017

  Summary:
    Originally proposed for object detection to address the extreme class
    imbalance between background and foreground regions. Focal Loss
    modifies standard cross-entropy by adding a factor (1-p_t)^gamma
    that down-weights the contribution of easy (well-classified) examples.
    At gamma=2, examples with p_t=0.9 receive 100x less weight than
    hard examples with p_t=0.1. Focal Loss has since been widely adopted
    in text classification for imbalanced datasets.

  Relevance to this project:
    The Veritas AI training pipeline implements Focal Loss (gamma=2.0) as
    its primary loss function, combined with per-class weights. This is
    the primary technique for handling the natural 4:4:1 class imbalance
    in the training data.

[Paper 5]
  Title:   "Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive
            NLP Tasks"
  Authors: Lewis, P., Perez, E., Piktus, A., et al.
  Year:    2020
  Venue:   NeurIPS 2020

  Summary:
    Proposed RAG, a hybrid architecture combining dense passage retrieval
    with a seq2seq language model. At inference time, the retriever fetches
    relevant passages from a knowledge base (using DPR — Dense Passage
    Retrieval), which are concatenated with the query and fed to the generator.
    This grounds the model's outputs in factual documents, reducing hallucination.

  Relevance to this project:
    The Veritas AI RAG pipeline is directly inspired by this architecture.
    DistilBERT serves as the dense encoder (analogous to DPR), FAISS provides
    the nearest-neighbor index, and an external LLM (Gemini/Groq/Ollama)
    serves as the generator. The pipeline enables evidence-grounded
    fact-checking beyond what the classifier alone can provide.

[Paper 6]
  Title:   "Billion-scale similarity search with GPUs"
  Authors: Johnson, J., Douze, M., & Jégou, H.
  Year:    2019
  Venue:   IEEE Transactions on Big Data

  Summary:
    Introduced FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search), a library for efficient
    similarity search and clustering of dense vectors. FAISS supports both
    exact and approximate nearest-neighbor search and scales to billions of
    vectors. The flat (exact) index used in Veritas AI computes true
    inner-product distances over all stored vectors, guaranteeing retrieval
    of the actual top-k most relevant passages.

  Relevance to this project:
    FAISS is the vector store backend for the RAG pipeline. Document embeddings
    produced by the DistilBERT encoder are stored and retrieved using FAISS's
    IndexFlatIP (inner product) index, with the index persisted to disk for
    reuse across server restarts.

[Paper 7]
  Title:   "FakeNewsNet: A Data Repository with News Content, Social
            Context, and Spatiotemporal Information for Studying Fake
            News on Social Media"
  Authors: Shu, K., Mahudeswaran, D., Wang, S., Lee, D., & Liu, H.
  Year:    2018
  Venue:   Big Data Journal

  Summary:
    Proposed a multi-dimensional fake news detection framework that integrates
    news content features, social context (user interactions, retweet graphs),
    and spatiotemporal diffusion patterns. The paper argues that content-only
    classifiers are insufficient and that propagation patterns are powerful
    signals of misinformation.

  Relevance to this project:
    This paper highlights a known limitation of content-only systems. Veritas AI
    is a content-only classifier in its current form. The findings motivate the
    inclusion of a RAG pipeline as a complementary evidence source and suggest
    that social graph features could be a valuable future enhancement.

[Paper 8]
  Title:   "Fake News Detection on Social Media: A Data Mining Perspective"
  Authors: Shu, K., Sliva, A., Wang, S., Tang, J., & Liu, H.
  Year:    2017
  Venue:   ACM SIGKDD Explorations Newsletter

  Summary:
    Comprehensive survey of fake news detection approaches. Classifies methods
    into knowledge-based (fact databases), style-based (linguistic patterns),
    propagation-based (network diffusion), and credibility-based (source trust).
    Provides taxonomy of news credibility and benchmark comparison.

  Relevance to this project:
    This taxonomy directly influenced the hybrid design of Veritas AI:
      Style-based → 152 linguistic deception patterns
      Knowledge-based → RAG fact-checking pipeline
      Deep learning → DistilBERT classifier
    The hybrid combination addresses weaknesses of any single approach.

[Paper 9]
  Title:   "FEVER: a Large-scale Dataset for Fact Extraction and Verification"
  Authors: Thorne, J., Vlachos, A., Christodoulopoulos, C., & Mittal, A.
  Year:    2018
  Venue:   NAACL 2018

  Summary:
    Introduced the FEVER dataset with 185,445 claims extracted from Wikipedia,
    each labeled as SUPPORTED, REFUTED, or NOT ENOUGH INFO. Provided a
    pipeline (claim decomposition + evidence retrieval + verdict) that is
    now a standard baseline for fact-checking systems.

  Relevance to this project:
    The FEVER pipeline architecture (retrieve-then-verify) directly informed
    the design of the RAG component. The "not enough info" category maps
    to the RAG system's explicit handling of insufficient context.

[Paper 10]
  Title:   "Detecting Fake News with BERT"
  Authors: Bhattacharjee, S., Talukdar, A., & Talukdar, B.
  Year:    2020
  Venue:   International Conference on Machine Learning and Applications

  Summary:
    Fine-tuned BERT on the Kaggle Fake News dataset achieving 99.7% binary
    accuracy. However, the paper notes that high accuracy on the Kaggle dataset
    does not generalise — the dataset contains stylistically distinct fake and
    real articles from specific sources, making classification too easy.

  Relevance to this project:
    This confirms the need for harder, more diverse datasets (LIAR, live news)
    and a more nuanced classification scheme. The 99.7% accuracy on Kaggle
    data is not reproducible on real-world diverse text. Our system deliberately
    uses cross-source data and a 3-class formulation to avoid this pitfall.

2.3 RESEARCH GAP
-----------------

Analysis of the literature reveals the following gaps that this project
specifically addresses:

  Gap 1: Binary Classification Granularity
    Almost all existing systems (BERT-based or otherwise) frame misinformation
    detection as a binary problem. Real-world content moderation requires
    severity-aware classification to enable proportional responses.

    → Veritas AI introduces three severity classes: Highly Deceptive,
      Misleading, and Legitimate.

  Gap 2: Lack of Explainability
    Most deep learning classifiers are black boxes. Stakeholders (editors,
    platform moderators, users) need to understand why content was flagged.
    Pure neural classifiers do not provide this.

    → Veritas AI combines the neural classifier with 152 explicit linguistic
      patterns that produce human-readable justification strings for every
      prediction.

  Gap 3: Static Knowledge / Temporal Decay
    All reviewed systems are evaluated on fixed datasets. No published system
    incorporates a mechanism for continuous model updating without manual
    intervention.

    → Veritas AI includes a scheduled pipeline that fetches live news,
      performs heuristic labeling, and retrains the model automatically.

  Gap 4: Retrieval-Only or Generation-Only Approaches
    Existing knowledge-grounded approaches either use pure retrieval (find
    similar articles) or pure generation (LLM prompting). RAG combining
    dense retrieval with LLM generation specifically for misinformation
    detection remains underexplored.

    → Veritas AI's RAG pipeline combines DistilBERT dense retrieval with
      a swappable LLM backend for evidence-grounded fact-checking.

      IF subject IN ["Government News", "Middle-east"]:
        label = 0  (Highly Deceptive)
      ELSE:
        label = 1  (Misleading)

    FOR each row in true_df:
      label = 2  (Legitimate)

    data = concat(fake_df, true_df)

  STEP 2: Merge LIAR Dataset (if --liar flag)
    liar_map = {0:0, 5:0, 2:1, 3:1, 4:2, 1:2}
    Download parquet from HuggingFace CDN (train + val + test splits)
    Map 6-class labels to 3-class using liar_map
    data = concat(data, liar_df)

  STEP 3: Merge Synthetic Data (if provided)
    data = concat(data, synthetic_df)

  STEP 4: Merge Live News (if provided)
    data = concat(data, live_news_df)

  STEP 5: Deduplicate
    data = drop_duplicates(subset=["text"])

  STEP 6: Balance Classes
    counts = {0: count_label_0, 1: count_label_1, 2: count_label_2}
    IF strategy == "undersample":
      target = min(counts.values())
    ELIF strategy == "oversample":
      target = max(counts.values())  [capped at 2x per class]
    ELIF strategy == "median":
      target = median(counts.values())

    FOR each class label:
      IF class_count < target:
        resample WITH replacement to target
      ELIF class_count > target:
        subsample WITHOUT replacement to target

  STEP 7: Shuffle and Split
    data = shuffle(data, seed=42)
    train, test = stratified_split(data, test_size=0.2, seed=42)

    write(train → "src/train_data.csv")
    write(test  → "src/test_data.csv")

3.3.3 Model Training Algorithm
--------------------------------

ALGORITHM: TrainDistilBERT()

  INPUT:
    train_data.csv (text, label)
    hyperparameters: epochs, batch_size, lr, dropout, gamma, patience

  OUTPUT:
    Trained model saved to models/

  STEP 1: Load Data
    data = read("src/train_data.csv")
    data = filter(len(text) > 10)

  STEP 2: Train/Val Split
    train_df, val_df = stratified_split(data, test_size=0.1, seed=42)

  STEP 3: Oversampling (if enabled)
    target = max count in train_df
    FOR each class with count < target:
      oversample (with replacement) to target

  STEP 4: Compute Class Weights
    class_weights = compute_class_weight("balanced", classes, labels)

  STEP 5: Initialize Model
    IF resume_from:
      model = DistilBertForSequenceClassification.from_pretrained(resume_from)
    ELSE:
      model = DistilBertForSequenceClassification.from_pretrained(
                "distilbert-base-uncased", num_labels=3)
    model.classifier.dropout = Dropout(p=dropout)

  STEP 6: Setup Optimizer with Differential LR
    backbone_params = all parameters NOT in "classifier"
    head_params     = all parameters IN "classifier"
    optimizer = AdamW([
      {params: backbone_params, lr: 2e-5},
      {params: head_params,     lr: 1e-4}  # 5x faster
    ], weight_decay=0.01)

  STEP 7: Setup LR Scheduler
    total_steps  = len(train_loader) * epochs
    warmup_steps = 0.1 * total_steps
    scheduler    = cosine_schedule_with_warmup(optimizer, warmup, total)

  STEP 8: Define Loss Function
    loss_fn = FocalLoss(gamma=2.0, weight=class_weights,
                        label_smoothing=0.1)

  STEP 9: Training Loop
    best_macro_f1    = -1
    patience_counter = 0

    FOR epoch IN range(epochs):
      # Training phase
      model.train()
      FOR each batch IN train_loader:
        WITH autocast (AMP):
          logits = model(input_ids, attention_mask)
          loss   = loss_fn(logits, labels)
        scaler.scale(loss).backward()
        clip_grad_norm(model.parameters(), max_norm=1.0)
        scaler.step(optimizer)
        scaler.update()
        scheduler.step()
        optimizer.zero_grad()

      # Validation phase
      model.eval()
      FOR each batch IN val_loader:
        WITH no_grad():
          logits = model(input_ids, attention_mask)
          preds  = argmax(logits, dim=1)

      macro_f1 = f1_score(all_labels, all_preds, average="macro")

      IF macro_f1 > best_macro_f1:
        best_macro_f1    = macro_f1
        patience_counter = 0
        model.save_pretrained("models/best_model_checkpoint")
      ELSE:
        patience_counter += 1
        IF patience_counter >= patience:
          BREAK  # Early stopping

  STEP 10: Save Final Model
    final = load("models/best_model_checkpoint")
    final.save_pretrained("models/")

3.3.4 Focal Loss Formula
--------------------------

  Standard Cross-Entropy:
    CE(p, y) = -log(p_y)

  where p_y = softmax probability assigned to the correct class y.

  Focal Loss (Lin et al., 2017):
    FL(p_t) = -(1 - p_t)^gamma * log(p_t)

  where:
    p_t    = probability of the correct class (confidence in the right answer)
    gamma  = focusing parameter (default: 2.0)

  The factor (1 - p_t)^gamma:
    - When p_t is high (easy example, e.g., 0.9):
        (1 - 0.9)^2 = 0.01  → loss reduced to 1% of CE
    - When p_t is low (hard example, e.g., 0.1):
        (1 - 0.1)^2 = 0.81  → loss reduced to 81% of CE

  In Veritas AI, Focal Loss is applied with:
    - gamma = 2.0
    - Per-class weight vector (balanced)
    - Label smoothing = 0.1

3.3.5 RAG Pipeline Algorithm
------------------------------

ALGORITHM: RAGIngest(documents)

  INPUT: List of text documents

  FOR each document:
    words = document.split(" ")
    IF len(words) <= chunk_size (150):
      chunks = [document]
    ELSE:
      step = chunk_size - chunk_overlap  (150 - 20 = 130)
      chunks = sliding windows of 150 words with step 130

    FOR each chunk:
      embedding = DistilBERT.encode(chunk)  # 768-dim vector
      embedding = L2_normalize(embedding)
      FAISS.add(embedding, chunk, metadata)

  FAISS.save_to_disk()

ALGORITHM: RAGQuery(question, top_k=5)

  INPUT: Natural language question string

  STEP 1: Encode Question
    q_emb = DistilBERT.encode(question)
    q_emb = L2_normalize(q_emb)

  STEP 2: Retrieve Top-k Chunks
    results = FAISS.search(q_emb, top_k)
    # Each result: {text, score (cosine similarity), metadata}

  STEP 3: Build Context
    context = ""
    FOR i, result IN enumerate(results):
      context += f"[{i}] (relevance {score*100}%) {result.text}"

  STEP 4: Build LLM Prompt
    prompt = SYSTEM_PROMPT + CONTEXT + QUESTION

  STEP 5: Generate Answer
    answer = LLM.generate(prompt, max_tokens=1024, temperature=0.2)

  STEP 6: Return Response
    RETURN {question, answer, sources: [{text, score, metadata}]}

3.3.7 Continuous Learning Pipeline
------------------------------------

ALGORITHM: ScheduledUpdate()

  Runs every NEWS_UPDATE_HOURS (default: 6) hours:

  STEP 1: Fetch Live News
    Sources:
      - RSS feeds (no API key required)

    Resume from: models/best_model_checkpoint

  STEP 4: Full Retrain
    Run data_preprocessing.py --liar --live-news auto
    Run train_model.py --focal-loss --oversample
    This retrains from scratch using ALL accumulated data.

4.2 ANALYSIS
-------------

4.2.1 Effect of Focal Loss

  Without Focal Loss (weighted CrossEntropy):
    The "Highly Deceptive" class (minority) was frequently predicted as
    "Misleading." The model learned to minimise total loss by performing well
    on the majority classes and sacrificing the minority class.

  With Focal Loss (gamma=2.0):
    The hard examples from the minority class receive amplified gradients.
    The model is forced to learn distinguishing features for "Highly Deceptive"
    content. Per-class F1 for the minority class improved by ~8 percentage points.

4.2.2 Effect of Differential Learning Rates

  With uniform learning rate (2e-5):
    The classifier head converges slowly — it has no pre-trained features to
    build on and must learn from scratch at the same rate as the stable backbone.

  With differential LR (backbone: 2e-5, head: 1e-4):
    The head reaches a good local optimum ~2-3 epochs faster. The backbone's
    pre-trained representations are preserved because its lower LR limits
    catastrophic forgetting. This is the standard practice for transformer
    fine-tuning and produced a 1-2% macro F1 improvement in our ablation.

4.2.3 Effect of Oversampling

  Without oversampling:
    When trained on the unbalanced dataset (4:4:1 approximate ratio), the model
    rarely predicted "Highly Deceptive" despite its literal presence in the data.

  With oversampling:
    The minority class is repeated to match the majority class count in the
    training set only. The validation set remains at natural distribution to
    give an honest early-stopping signal. Recall for "Highly Deceptive" improved
    significantly.

4.2.4 Class Confusion Analysis

  The most common misclassification is between "Misleading" and "Highly Deceptive,"
  which is expected and arguably acceptable. The boundary between exaggerated
  framing and outright fabrication is genuinely ambiguous even for human annotators.

  "Legitimate" content is rarely misclassified as "Highly Deceptive" (the most
  dangerous error), indicating that the system has a strong boundary between
  credible and severely deceptive content.

4.2.5 Macro F1 vs Accuracy

  Accuracy on the balanced test set (~90%) is comparable to the macro F1 (~0.90).
  This is expected on a balanced test set. On a naturally imbalanced real-world
  corpus (with more "Legitimate" articles than "Highly Deceptive"), accuracy would
  be higher while macro F1 would remain more informative about minority class
  performance.

  Macro F1 is the primary reported metric because:
    - It equally weights all three classes
    - It captures both precision and recall
    - It is invariant to class imbalance in the test set

4.5 INFERENCE
--------------

Based on the experimental results and analysis, the following inferences
are drawn:

  Inference 1:
    Transformer-based fine-tuning (DistilBERT) significantly outperforms
    classical feature engineering approaches for misinformation detection.
    The contextual understanding afforded by pre-trained attention mechanisms
    is critical for detecting subtle forms of deception.

  Inference 2:
    Focal Loss is an effective technique for handling class imbalance in
    text classification tasks, reducing the tendency of the model to ignore
    minority classes. It should be preferred over weighted CrossEntropy
    when the minority-to-majority ratio exceeds 1:3.

  Inference 3:
    Early stopping on macro F1 (rather than validation loss) leads to better
    generalization across all classes, particularly the minority class. Validation
    loss alone can continue improving even as minority class F1 degrades.

  Inference 4:
    The addition of the LIAR dataset (political statements, domain-diverse)
    improves generalization beyond the Kaggle dataset's limited style space.
    Cross-source training is essential for real-world robustness.

  Inference 5:
    The RAG pipeline is most valuable for specific factual claims (e.g., medical
    statistics, historical events) where the vector store contains relevant
    passages. It provides less benefit for opinion-framing or emotional manipulation
    patterns, which are better captured by the classifier and linguistic rules.

  Inference 6:
    The continuous learning pipeline significantly extends the operational
    lifetime of the deployed model by keeping it updated with emerging
    misinformation patterns without requiring manual retraining.

5.1 ADVANTAGES
---------------

  1. Severity-Aware Classification
     Unlike binary systems, Veritas AI provides three actionable severity levels.
     Content moderators can apply proportionate responses: remove "Highly Deceptive"
     content, add warnings to "Misleading" content, and pass "Legitimate" content.

  2. Explainable AI
     Every prediction includes a list of human-readable reasons and a recommendation.
     This builds user trust, satisfies emerging AI explainability regulations, and
     allows human reviewers to quickly assess the system's reasoning.

  3. Efficient Model Architecture
     DistilBERT is 40% smaller and 60% faster than BERT with 97% of its accuracy.
     This makes real-time inference feasible without GPU hardware, reducing
     infrastructure costs significantly.

  4. Continuous Learning
     The automated scheduled pipeline ensures the model remains current without
     manual intervention. This is a critical advantage in the rapidly evolving
     misinformation landscape.

  5. Hybrid Detection
     Combining neural classification with linguistic pattern rules provides
     complementary strengths: the neural model handles semantic and contextual
     signals, while patterns handle known, specific deception markers explicitly.

  6. Evidence-Grounded Fact-Checking (RAG)
     The optional RAG pipeline allows the system to ground its fact-checking
     in retrieved documentary evidence, reducing reliance on patterns learned
     during training and providing source citations.

  7. Multi-Provider LLM Support
     The RAG pipeline supports four LLM backends (Gemini, Groq, HuggingFace,
     Ollama). Ollama supports fully local, offline inference with no API keys
     or costs, making the system deployable in privacy-sensitive environments.

  8. Production-Ready
     Full Docker support, environment-based configuration, REST API,
     health check endpoint, startup model validation, and graceful error
     handling make Veritas AI immediately deployable in production.

  9. Open Standards and Open Source Stack
     Built entirely on open-source libraries (PyTorch, HuggingFace, FAISS,
     FastAPI). No proprietary dependencies in the core classification pipeline.

  10. Scalable API Design
      FastAPI's async architecture and Uvicorn ASGI server support high
      concurrent request loads with low overhead.

5.2 LIMITATIONS
----------------

  1. English-Only
     DistilBERT-base-uncased is trained on English text only. The system cannot
     classify misinformation in other languages without retraining on a
     multilingual model (e.g., XLM-RoBERTa).

  2. 128-Token Input Limit
     The default tokenisation truncates text at 128 tokens (~100 words). Long
     articles are truncated, potentially losing key information at the end.
     This can be increased to 256 tokens at 2x computational cost.

  3. Content-Only Analysis
     The system analyzes only the text content of articles. Social signals
     (share counts, user reputation, propagation patterns) that are known
     to be strong misinformation indicators are not incorporated.

  4. Heuristic Live News Labels
     Labels assigned to live news articles during automatic ingestion are
     heuristic (based on API confidence scores), not human-verified. This
     introduces noise into the continuous learning pipeline.

  5. RAG Requires Manual Document Ingestion
     The RAG vector store must be populated manually via the /rag/ingest
     endpoint. There is no automated indexing of external fact databases.

  6. No Image/Video Analysis
     The system processes text only. Multimedia misinformation (deepfakes,
     manipulated images used in misleading contexts) is outside its scope.

  7. Satire Edge Cases
     Satirical content using the same linguistic patterns as conspiracy theories
     can occasionally be misclassified as "Highly Deceptive" or "Misleading"
     if the satirical intent is not apparent from text alone.

  8. Model Interpretability
     While the linguistic pattern layer provides explainability, the DistilBERT
     component itself is a black-box transformer. Attention weights provide
     limited interpretability but are not shown to end users.

CONCLUSION
-----------

This report presented Veritas AI, a hybrid misinformation detection system
developed as a Semester VI minor project. The system addresses three key gaps
in the existing literature: binary classification granularity, static knowledge,
and lack of explainability.

The core classifier fine-tunes DistilBERT-base-uncased on approximately 51,000
labeled samples from three sources: the Kaggle Fake/Real News dataset, the LIAR
benchmark, and synthetically generated examples. A three-class severity taxonomy
(Highly Deceptive, Misleading, Legitimate) provides more actionable output than
binary classification.

Advanced training techniques — Focal Loss, differential learning rates, cosine
scheduling with warmup, label smoothing, auto-oversampling, and macro F1-based
early stopping — result in a best model achieving a macro F1 score of 0.9008
(v6), outperforming the baseline by approximately 9 F1 percentage points.

The hybrid prediction engine combines the DistilBERT classifier with 152
linguistic deception patterns, providing human-readable justifications alongside
every classification. An optional RAG pipeline enables evidence-grounded
fact-checking using DistilBERT-based dense retrieval (FAISS), a swappable LLM
backend, and overlapping word-level chunking.

A continuous learning pipeline — powered by APScheduler, live news fetching
from RSS/GDELT/NewsAPI/Google Fact Check, and both incremental and full
retraining cycles — ensures the system remains current without manual
intervention.

The complete system is deployed via a FastAPI REST server with Docker support,
served alongside a multi-page HTML/CSS/JS frontend, and is configurable through
environment variables without code changes.

In conclusion, Veritas AI demonstrates that a carefully designed hybrid system,
trained with modern class-imbalance techniques and augmented with retrieval-based
fact-checking, can achieve high accuracy on the challenging task of nuanced
misinformation severity classification, while remaining practical to deploy and
operate.

     non-English classification. This is particularly valuable given that
     misinformation in regional languages is underserved by current tools.

  2. Longer Context Window
     Replace the 128-token truncation with a sliding window approach (overlapping
     segments of the full article aggregated by voting) or replace DistilBERT
     with Longformer/BigBird which support up to 4096 tokens natively.

  3. Browser Extension
     Develop a Chrome/Firefox extension that calls the API transparently as
     users browse, inserting non-intrusive severity badges into news article
     pages in real time.

  4. Social Graph Features
     Incorporate propagation network features (retweet graph, user credibility
     scores) as additional signals alongside text content, following the
     FakeNewsNet framework.

  5. Auto-Indexing of Fact Databases
     Implement automatic ingestion of publicly available fact-check databases
     (Snopes, PolitiFact, FactCheck.org) into the FAISS RAG vector store,
     enabling fully automated evidence retrieval.

  6. Human-in-the-Loop Feedback
     Add a user feedback mechanism to the web interface, allowing users to
     flag incorrect predictions. These corrections, after quality filtering,
     can be incorporated into the training pipeline as high-quality labels.

  7. RLHF Fine-Tuning
     Apply Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback on the LLM component of
     the RAG pipeline to align generated fact-check verdicts with human
     preferences and improve factual accuracy.

  8. Multimodal Analysis
     Extend the system to analyze images and videos accompanying text, detecting
     deepfakes, out-of-context imagery, and manipulated media — addressing a
     major category of modern misinformation.

  9. Real-Time Social Media Monitoring
     Develop a streaming pipeline (Apache Kafka or similar) to process live
     social media posts at scale, enabling real-time trend detection and alerts.

  10. Fine-Grained Confidence Calibration
      Apply post-hoc calibration techniques (temperature scaling, Platt scaling)
      to ensure the model's confidence scores are well-calibrated probability
      estimates rather than overconfident softmax outputs.

A.1 COMPLETE PREDICTION FLOW (Detailed)

  POST /predict
       |
       v
  [API Request Validation]
  - Is text empty?         → 400 Bad Request
  - Is len(text) > 5000?  → 400 Bad Request
       |
       v
  [Tokenization]
  tokenizer = DistilBertTokenizer.from_pretrained("distilbert-base-uncased")
  tokens = tokenizer(text, truncation=True, padding="max_length",
                     max_length=128, return_tensors="pt")
  output: {input_ids: [batch, 128], attention_mask: [batch, 128]}
       |
       v
  [DistilBERT Forward Pass]
  model = DistilBertForSequenceClassification (fine-tuned)
  logits = model(input_ids, attention_mask)  → shape: [1, 3]
       |
       v
  [Softmax]
  probabilities = softmax(logits)  → [p_highly_deceptive, p_misleading, p_legitimate]
  predicted_class = argmax(probabilities)
  confidence = max(probabilities) * 100
       |
       v
  [Linguistic Pattern Matching]
  FOR each pattern IN 152_patterns:
    IF pattern.regex.search(text):
      reasons.append(pattern.explanation)
      IF pattern.type NOT IN detected_types:
        content_type = pattern.type
       |
       v
  [RAG (if initialised)]
  IF _rag is not None:
    rag_result = _rag.query(text, top_k=3)
    IF rag_result.answer:
      reasons.append("Fact-check: " + rag_result.answer[:200])
       |
       v
  [Response Assembly]
  severity       = LABEL_NAMES[predicted_class]
  recommendation = RECOMMENDATIONS[predicted_class]
  RETURN {severity, confidence, type, reasons, recommendation}

A.2 TRAINING LOOP FLOWCHART

  START EPOCH
       |
       v
  [Training Phase]
  FOR each batch:
    → Forward pass (with AMP autocast)
    → Focal Loss calculation
    → Backward pass (with gradient scaling)
    → Gradient clipping (max_norm=1.0)
    → Optimizer step
    → Scheduler step
    → Zero gradients
       |
       v
  [Validation Phase]
  FOR each val batch:
    → Forward pass (no_grad, with AMP autocast)
    → Collect predictions and labels
       |
       v
  [Metrics]
  macro_f1 = f1_score(all_labels, all_preds, average="macro")
  per_class_f1 = classification_report(...)
       |
       v
  macro_f1 > best_macro_f1?
    YES → Save checkpoint → Reset patience_counter
    NO  → patience_counter += 1
          patience_counter >= patience? → STOP (early stopping)
       |
       v
  END EPOCH → NEXT EPOCH

A.3 RAG INGESTION FLOWCHART

  INPUT: texts (list of strings), metadatas (optional)
       |
       v
  FOR each (text, metadata):
    words = text.split()
    len(words) <= 150?
      YES → chunks = [text]
      NO  → sliding window (size=150, step=130)
              FOR start in range(0, len(words), 130):
                chunk = words[start : start+150]
                chunks.append(" ".join(chunk))
       |
       v
  FOR each chunk:
    embedding = DistilBERT.encode(chunk)  # CLS token, 768-dim
    embedding = L2_normalize(embedding)
    FAISS.add(embedding)
    metadata_store.append({chunk, metadata, doc_idx, chunk_idx})
       |
       v
  FAISS.write_index("rag_store/index.faiss")
  JSON.dump(metadata_store, "rag_store/metadata.json")
  OUTPUT: number of chunks added
