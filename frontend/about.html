What Veritas is, why it was built, and the team behind it.

"Misinformation spreads faster than corrections. Veritas was built to give people a tool to pause, evaluate, and think critically before accepting or sharing what they read online."

What is Veritas?

Veritas is a system designed to analyze written content and identify how misleading the information might be. Instead of simply labeling something as true or false, the system evaluates the level and severity of misinformation — returning one of three classifications along with a confidence score.

By analyzing text patterns, language context, and claim structure, Veritas helps users better understand whether a piece of content may be Misleading, Highly Deceptive, or Legitimate.

How the system works

01

Paste your text

Any written content — a news headline, social post, or full article — up to 5,000 characters.

02

Model analyzes it

A transformer model evaluates linguistic patterns, claim structure, and deceptive framing signals.

03

You get a verdict

A severity rating, confidence score, type classification, and plain-language explanation.

Purpose of the project

The spread of misinformation online can influence opinions, health decisions, and political outcomes. Veritas was created as a final-year project to explore how machine learning can be applied to help identify misleading information more effectively.

By providing clear severity levels rather than a binary verdict, the system encourages nuanced critical thinking and helps users better evaluate the reliability of digital content before acting on it or sharing it further.
