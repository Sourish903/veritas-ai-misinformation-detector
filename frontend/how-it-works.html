Understanding the severity levels

Every analysis returns one of three severity ratings. Here's what each one means.

Legitimate

The content is factually sound and accurately framed. Claims are supported, context is present, and language is measured without misleading intent.

Common examplesWell-sourced news reports · Peer-reviewed findings accurately described · Official statements quoted in context
Misleading

The content distorts facts through exaggeration, missing context, or selective framing. Not outright false, but creates a false impression.

Common examplesHeadlines that strip important nuance · Statistics presented without context · Anecdotal evidence framed as universal
Highly Deceptive

The content is designed to deceive. Contains fabricated facts, conspiracy framing, or deliberate manipulation with little to no basis in verified reality.

Common examplesFabricated government cover-up claims · Medical misinformation with fake citations · Coordinated disinformation narratives

About the model

What's actually happening under the hood when you submit text.

🧠

Transformer architecture

Built on a fine-tuned transformer model trained on large corpora of labeled misinformation and verified news content.

🎯

Multi-label classification

Rather than a binary true/false output, the model produces a probability distribution across severity tiers.

📐

Confidence calibration

The confidence score reflects how strongly the model distinguishes the predicted class from alternatives — not just raw probability.

⚠️

Limitations

No model is perfect. Results should be used as a starting point for critical thinking, not as a final verdict. Always cross-check important claims.
