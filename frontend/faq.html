Everything you need to know about how Veritas works.

The basics
What is Veritas? ▼
Veritas is a tool that analyzes written text and classifies how misleading or deceptive the information might be. Unlike simple fact-checkers, it evaluates severity on a three-level scale — Legitimate, Misleading, and Highly Deceptive — rather than giving a binary true/false verdict.
How does Veritas work? ▼
The system uses a fine-tuned transformer model trained on labeled misinformation datasets. When you paste text, it evaluates linguistic patterns, claim structure, emotional language, and known deception signals — then returns a severity rating and confidence score.
What type of text can I analyze? ▼
You can analyze news articles, social media posts, WhatsApp forwards, emails, blog posts, or any written claim. The analyzer supports up to 5,000 characters per submission.
Do I need to create an account? ▼
No. Veritas requires no sign-up or account. Just paste your text and click Analyze.
Results and accuracy
What do the severity levels mean? ▼
There are three levels. Legitimate means the content is factually sound with no deceptive framing. Misleading means the content contains inaccurate framing, missing context, or exaggeration that distorts the truth. Highly Deceptive means the content is deliberately designed to mislead, with fabricated facts or conspiracy framing.
What is a confidence score? ▼
The confidence score (0–100%) reflects how strongly the model distinguishes the predicted severity class from the alternatives. A higher score means the model is more certain about its verdict. A lower score suggests the content sits on the boundary between two categories.
How accurate is Veritas? ▼
The model is trained on labeled datasets and performs well on common misinformation patterns. However, no model is perfect — results should be used as a starting point for critical thinking, not as a final verdict. Always cross-check important claims with authoritative sources.
Can Veritas detect all types of fake news? ▼
Veritas is designed to identify misleading language patterns and deceptive framing. It performs best on text-based claims and articles. It may be less reliable on very short snippets, highly technical content, or languages other than English.
Privacy and data
Does Veritas store my text? ▼
No. The text you submit is sent to the analysis server only for processing and is not stored permanently. Your analysis history is saved locally in your own browser using localStorage — it never leaves your device.
Where is my analysis history stored? ▼
Your history is stored in your browser's localStorage. It persists across page refreshes but stays on your device. You can clear it at any time using the "Clear all" button in the history section on the home page.
Using the tool
Can I analyze long articles? ▼
Yes, up to 5,000 characters. For longer articles, paste the most critical section — typically the headline, introduction, and key claims — for the most relevant analysis.
Can I export or share my results? ▼
Yes. Once you have a result, you can copy it as a formatted text summary using the Copy button, or export it as a print-ready PDF report using the Export PDF button — both appear on the result panel.
Why is misinformation detection important? ▼
Misleading content spreads rapidly online and can influence opinions, health decisions, and political views. Tools like Veritas help people pause and evaluate content critically before sharing or acting on it — promoting more reliable information ecosystems.
